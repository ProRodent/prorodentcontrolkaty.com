The kinds of damage rodents cause.

Rodents gnaw constantly. In a house, that means wires, ducts, pipes, insulation and wood. In a garage or vehicle, it means cables, hoses and stored goods. The damage can be small or sudden, and it rarely repairs itself.

The first step is to see it clearly. We photograph each area, measure the extent and describe what needs replacing, repairing or watching.

Common repairs and why they matter.

Damage.What it causes.Typical repair.
Torn flexible ducts.Cooled air leaks into the attic.Replace the section and seal the joints.
Flattened or soiled insulation.Less heat resistance, smell.Remove and install new insulation.
Gnawed vent screens.Open entry and wasted air.Install metal screens or covers.

Chewed wire is a safety matter. Leave it untouched, and switch off that circuit only if you can manage it safely. We note it and leave the repair to a licensed electrician.

The right order of work.

Repair comes last for good reason. If animals are still active, they chew new work. If entries remain open, more arrive. The sequence we follow runs from inspection through trapping, then sealing and cleanup, with repair at the end.

  • Confirm the animals are gone with a return check.
  • Close entries with exclusion work.
  • Remove soiled material in the cleanup visit.
  • Repair the damage and test the result.

Heat, humidity and repairs.

In a hot, humid climate, ducts and insulation work hard. A torn duct can drip condensation or push cooled air into the attic, and damp insulation grows musty. We check for moisture while we are there and mention anything unrelated to rodents that needs another trade.

How we price and report.

You receive a written description of each damaged item, a photograph and a price. Some customers repair a few items and do the rest themselves. Others hand us everything. Either is fine.

If you plan an insurance claim, ask your insurer what documents it wants. We can supply photographs and a list of findings.

Describe to the dispatcher.

  • Pictures of gnawed insulation, ductwork or cables.

First moves at home.

  • Check that the side gate swings open, and let us know if a dog runs loose in the yard.
  • Leave shrubs and vines touching the walls alone until they have been seen.
  • Put a flashlight somewhere handy for shadowy corners.
  • Take photos of burrows, pellets and nest scraps before anything is covered over or moved.

Safety checklist for rodent droppings

Everything here repeats guidance from public health bodies, so any household can use it whether or not it ever books a service.

  • No matter how small the mess, skip dry methods and start with a disinfectant solution.
  • Shut-in rooms hold stale air, so throw open doors and windows and wait outside about thirty minutes.
  • At minimum wear gloves while working, and wash when the job is finished.
  • Disinfect first, collect afterward, once the soaked waste has stood for about five minutes.
  • Combine wipes and debris in a single bag, knot it and discard it, covering your hands near any body.
  • We offer no medical claims. If fever, muscle aches or labored breathing follow contact with waste, consult a physician.

Repair questions from Katy callers.

Why repair after sealing?

If entries are open, animals return and chew the new work. Sealing first protects the repair.

Do you fix electrical wiring?

No. If we see gnawed wiring, we note it and photograph it, and a licensed electrician should repair it. We can describe what to ask for.

Does damaged insulation matter in this climate?

Yes. Flattened or soiled insulation lowers the barrier against attic heat and may raise cooling costs.

Can I get a price before work?

Yes. Each repair is priced as a separate line after the walk-through.

Do you work with insurance?

We can provide photos and a written description of what we found. Whether a claim applies is between you and your insurer.
