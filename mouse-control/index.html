Why mice settle into Katy homes.

House mice want three things: warmth or coolness, shelter and a food source within a short run. A wall void behind a pantry offers all three. So do boxes of seasonal gear in a garage and the loose insulation around an attic hatch.

In a hot, humid climate the pull works in both directions. A cooled house can look as welcoming in July as a heated one does in January, and a bout of heavy rain can nudge animals out of low ground and toward drier cover. Neither factor needs an old house or a dirty one.

Because mice move mostly at night, many owners learn about them only when the house goes quiet. Listen near the pantry, the water heater closet and the garage wall that backs onto the kitchen.

Reading the small clues before a trap goes down.

We begin at the spot with the most evidence and work outward. New droppings, oily smears on a baseboard and chewed carton corners sketch a route, and every route ends at a way in. Setting traps without that map takes out a few animals while the cause stays open.

  • Look beneath kitchen and bathroom sinks, behind the oven and fridge, and in the closet that houses the water heater.
  • Study the garage door corners, its bottom rubber seal and the joint where the wall meets the slab.
  • Go over the attic hatch, vent perimeters and any wall penetration for pipes or wires.
  • Review the foundation vents on whichever side of the house stays dampest after rain.

Catch the animals first, then shut the holes.

Snap traps and closed bait stations are laid out along the evidence trail, high or hidden where kids and pets cannot get to them. Mice travel hugging walls and test anything new, which is why a trap flush with a baseboard beats one in the middle of the floor.

Sealing begins when early catches reveal which animals are active. Stuffing steel wool alone fails, because mice tug it out. Each opening is fitted with flashing, mesh and sealant, and the exclusion page describes the materials.

What you can do in the meantime.

Keep the broom away from dry droppings and keep any loose bait outside the house, because sweeping lifts dust into the air. Should you want to tidy before our visit, follow the damp method described on the CDC page linked below.

In this climate, food storage is important. Keep pantry staples and kibble in lidded tubs and pick up pet bowls at night. Such habits cannot clear an infestation alone, yet they remove the reward that lures mice back.

After the traps go quiet.

Success looks like quiet nights, no new droppings and traps that stay empty. A heavily fouled attic or wall void needs attic and crawl space decontamination, and gnawed cable or insulation belongs to rodent damage repair.

Not sure whether mice or something larger is behind the noise? Start with an inspection, and you will leave with a named animal and a plan.

Quick steps for the wait.

  • Close the door on the worst-affected room and leave it closed.
  • Dry pellets stay put; sweeping them lifts dust you do not want in your lungs.
  • Hardware-store bait can send a poisoned animal off to die in a duct or wall cavity.
  • Note the clock time when scratching starts, because timing says a lot about the species.

Safety checklist for rodent droppings

Going slowly at the start keeps fine dust out of your airways.

  • Never dry-sweep, because a broom flings fine particles up to face height.
  • A garage, shed or crawl space that has sat closed should get roughly half an hour of through-draft before you enter.
  • Start with gloves on and end with a hand wash.
  • Soak, wait, wipe: that is the sequence, with a five-minute pause in the middle.
  • Choose a bag that seals firmly, and use gloves or a tool as a barrier against the carcass.
  • Fever, aches or breathlessness following contact with waste belong with a clinic rather than an exterminator.

Mouse questions from Katy callers.

How small a hole can a mouse use?

Picture a slit that a ballpoint pen only just enters. Openings like that appear around drain pipes, behind dryer vents, along door edges and wherever cable passes into a wall.

Do mice come in during summer heat?

They can. A cooled house is attractive in July, much as a warm one is in winter, and a bout of heavy rain can send mice looking for dry cover. Gaps that go unnoticed all spring may become doors by late summer.

Is it all right to use hardware-store bait first?

Retail bait comes in loaded stations, and a mouse that eats from one can crawl into a wall and die. Speak with the technician before putting any out.

When will the scratching end?

The answer turns on the mouse count and on whether each entry hole gets closed. We offer a realistic schedule once the walk-through is finished.

Will anyone take a mouse call overnight?

Yes. The phone is staffed all night, and a technician may arrive the same day if there is a gap in the schedule.
