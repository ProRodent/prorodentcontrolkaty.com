Warning signs inside and out.

Signs like these deserve quick attention, and they tell us if a visit can safely wait for morning.

  • Attic noise following a stretch of heavy rain.
  • A rotting smell with nothing in sight to explain it.
  • Torn insulation or crushed flexible duct.
  • Droppings next to the air handler or water heater.
  • Worn tracks along a fence line or foundation.

Rental units, shops and shared walls.

If you rent, tell the landlord about the problem early. Rodents travel via party walls, plumbing chases and attic runs, so a single unit treated alone may see animals return from the next. A joint plan holds better.

For shops and storerooms, the questions are about deliveries and stock. Boxes on the floor, a propped door and an open dumpster are what we look at first. The commercial page describes scheduled checks and written notes.

What to do during the wait, and what to leave alone

Return to this routine each time you find pellets, urine marks or torn nesting inside the house.

  • The first rule is simple: no brushing or vacuuming of anything dry.
  • Unseen hazards can linger in stale air, so open the space, step away and give it half an hour.
  • Gloves guard you while you work, and soap guards you once you stop.
  • Soak the waste thoroughly, let five minutes go by, then take it up with towels.
  • Tie the bag shut and put it with ordinary household trash. Lifting a dead rodent with bare fingers is never acceptable.
  • Should fever, aches or breathing trouble appear over the next several weeks, see a doctor and mention the waste exposure. Pests are our trade, not medicine.
