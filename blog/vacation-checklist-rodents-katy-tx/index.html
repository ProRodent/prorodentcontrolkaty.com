Vacation Checklist: Keep Rodents Out While Away in Katy

A house that sits quiet for a week or two is easy to move into. One checklist on the way out and another on the way back guards the pantry, the garage and the car.

Rodents move into quiet houses. Two quick routines, one before departure and one on return, pay off. The most important steps are to clear food and trash, close entry points and check vehicles and the garage. Here is the list.

Before you leave.

  • Take out all trash and wipe counters and the stovetop.
  • Move pantry staples, pet food and birdseed into sealed plastic or glass.
  • Empty and wipe water bowls and fix any dripping faucet.
  • Check the garage door seal and close the door fully.
  • Lift boxes and totes a few inches off the garage floor.
  • Collect fruit that has dropped and brush away seed under the feeders.

Half an hour of this removes most of the reasons an animal would stay.

Walk the outside.

Take a look at the places you normally walk past: vents, collars around pipes, the corners of the garage door, window screens and any branch that rests on the roof.

Vehicles and storage.

A car left alone for a week is a ready-made nest. If you can, ask a neighbor to run the engine briefly or move the vehicle. Remove snacks from the cabin and glance under the hood before departing. Read our vehicle page for the signs.

Lift stored items off the floor in the garage and shed. If you have a trailer or camper, close lockers and remove food.

Who checks while you are away.

Ask a neighbor or sitter to look in once or twice. Explain what to look for, such as pellets on counters or floors, chewed packaging, fresh noises and strange smells. They should leave droppings untouched and phone you, and you can hand them our number.

When you come back.

Before you unload, throw the doors and windows open for half an hour, then tour every room. Check the pantry, the garage, the attic hatch and the area behind appliances. If you find droppings, stop. Do not sweep or vacuum. Glove up, soak the droppings in disinfectant, wait, wipe and bag. The CDC steps below show how.

If signs turn up, an inspection identifies the animal and its way in.

Fixing what you find.

Gaps need closing with metal and mesh, as our exclusion page describes. Animals inside need trapping first. Soiled space may need cleanup.

For longer trips.

If you will be away for a month or more, add a few steps. Ask someone to check the house every week, and have them run taps and flush toilets to keep traps and drains from drying out. Leave the thermostat set so the house stays cool, and check the attic hatch and the garage on each visit.

If anything turns up, they should photograph it and call you instead of cleaning. That keeps the evidence intact and keeps them safe from dust.

When to call.

Call if you return to droppings, chewed packaging, a nest or a smell. Tell us how long you were away and where you saw sign. A person answers at any hour.

Safety checklist for rodent droppings

Read through every line before handling anything, because a short pause spares your lungs.

  • Hurried cleaning makes dust, so go slowly and leave the brush alone.
  • Shut-in rooms hold stale air, so throw open doors and windows and wait outside about thirty minutes.
  • Single-use or reusable gloves both do the job, so long as the gloves and your hands are washed after.
  • Saturate droppings along with the spot below them using household disinfectant or a label-strength bleach solution. Leave it for about five minutes before blotting with paper towels.
  • Everything you collected goes into a closed bag ahead of the bin, and a carcass needs gloves.
  • Fever, aches or breathlessness following contact with waste belong with a clinic rather than an exterminator.

Answers in a line or two.

How long is too long to leave a house empty?

Rodents can move in within days. A week or more raises the odds, so the checklist matters for any trip.

Should I leave pet food out for a sitter?

Only what the pet needs for each meal, and pick up the bowl after. Store the rest in a lidded tub.

Is it fine to leave the garage door closed?

Yes, but check the seal first. A closed door with a gap is still an open door.

Who can look in on the house while I travel?

A neighbor or house sitter can watch for droppings, new sounds and odd smells, provided you hand over a short list and our number.

What do I do on return if I find droppings?

Open the space for about half an hour, wear gloves, wet the droppings, wipe and bag them, then call.
