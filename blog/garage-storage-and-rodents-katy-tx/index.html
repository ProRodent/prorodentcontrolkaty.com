Garage Storage and Rodents: A Katy Homeowner's Guide

In many homes the garage holds more stuff than any other room, and it gets the least attention. That combination suits rodents, and a short routine can keep it from turning into a nest.

A garage draws rodents because it combines shelter, quiet and soft material. A worn door seal and a few boxes against the wall are enough. The fix is a short monthly check, a few shelving changes and sealed containers for anything edible.

What makes a garage attractive.

Garages are shaded, often unconditioned and rarely visited. Cardboard, old towels, seasonal decorations and cushions are ideal nesting material. Pet food, birdseed and lawn seed are a meal. Add a gap at the door, and the garage becomes both shelter and restaurant.

The heat matters too. Many garages in this climate stay very hot in summer, which can push animals toward cooler corners behind shelves and under stored items. In mild or rainy weather, the same spots look dry and sheltered.

A brief monthly walkaround.

Put a reminder on your phone for the first weekend of the month. With the door open and a flashlight, work around the room.

  • Look for daylight along the bottom and corners of the overhead door.
  • Check the side door sweep and the threshold.
  • Glance behind the freezer, water heater and shelving.
  • Raise a few totes and check beneath them for pellets or shredded material.
  • Check the walls for gnaw marks near pipes and wires.

If everything is clean, you are done in minutes. If something is wrong, you caught it early.

Closing the door properly.

The overhead door is the biggest opening in the room. Check that the bottom seal sits tight on the slab and that the corners meet the frame. A seal that has cracked, flattened or pulled loose is cheap to replace, and the repair often removes the main entry.

Side doors need a sweep, and any gap around pipes and wires should be closed with metal and sealant. See exclusion work and our garage page for the details.

If you find sign.

Droppings, shredded paper, chewed totes and a musty smell all mean activity. Do not sweep or vacuum. Open the door for half an hour to air the space, put gloves on and soak droppings in disinfectant before any wiping, which is the CDC approach.

Then call. Trapping takes the animals out, sealing shuts their way in, and cleanup restores the room. If a vehicle is stored there, check under the hood too.

Cables, openers and appliances.

Garage door openers, spare freezers and workbenches hold wiring that rodents may chew. Damaged cable is a fire risk, so do not use a freezer or opener with chewed insulation. Have an electrician check it and tell us what you found.

When to call.

Call if you see droppings on shelves, hear scratching in the walls near the garage or smell something unpleasant. Tell us how the door meets the slab and what you store, and the first visit goes quickly.

Before we arrive: careful cleanup

Agencies broadly agree on how to clean up, and the overlap is condensed here.

  • Dampen first, wipe second, and never sweep dry.
  • Shut-in rooms hold stale air, so throw open doors and windows and wait outside about thirty minutes.
  • Put on rubber, vinyl or comparable gloves for soiled items, and afterward lather your hands with soap and warm water.
  • Disinfect first, collect afterward, once the soaked waste has stood for about five minutes.
  • Everything you collected goes into a closed bag ahead of the bin, and a carcass needs gloves.
  • Flu-type symptoms after a cleanup should be mentioned to a physician, since diagnosing them is not our work.

Follow-up questions.

Why do rodents go for garages?

They offer shade, quiet and soft material for nests. A worn door seal makes them easy to enter, and stored food makes them worth entering.

Are plastic tubs really better than cardboard?

Yes. Rodents chew cardboard for nesting and cannot easily chew through thick plastic with a tight lid.

How much space should I leave along the wall?

A hand's width or more lets you see the wall base and discourages nesting behind boxes.

Is pet food in the garage a problem?

It can be. Use a metal or hard plastic bin with a tight lid and pick up spills.

What if I find droppings on a shelf?

Leave them. Do not sweep. Wet them with disinfectant, wait, wipe and bag, following the CDC steps at the foot of the page.
