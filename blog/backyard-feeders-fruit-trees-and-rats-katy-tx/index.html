Feeders, Fruit Trees and Rats in Katy Backyards

Fruit trees and bird feeders make a backyard pleasant, and they can make it a restaurant. A few small habits keep the garden and cut the rats.

Fruit trees, feeders, pet bowls and compost can all feed rats. The good news is that small habits make a big difference: pick up fallen fruit, lid your seed, bring bowls in at night and keep limbs off the roof. You can keep the garden and cut the visits.

What draws rats to a yard.

Rats want food, water and cover. A backyard may offer all three without any neglect. Windfall fruit, spilled seed, a pet bowl and a dripping hose are common. A woodpile, dense shrubs or a stack of pots provide cover.

Roof rats are climbers and favor fences, vines, wires and limbs that lead to the eaves. Norway rats burrow beside slabs, sheds and fence posts. Either may show up on the Gulf Coast, and the yard tells you which.

Cutting the route to the roof.

A rat can walk along a fence, a cable or a limb to reach the roofline. Limbs that touch shingles are a bridge, and a vine on a wall is a ladder. Trim them back, and ask a tree service for large limbs.

Then look at the roof edge. A gap at a soffit or a vent without a screen is the way in. Our exclusion page describes how we close them with metal and mesh.

Signs of rats.

  • Gnawed fruit with wide, deep tooth marks.
  • Droppings larger than a grain of rice, in groups.
  • Burrow holes about two inches across beside a slab or shed.
  • Dark, greasy rub marks along a fence rail or pipe.
  • Thumping on the roof just after dusk.

Two or three of these together are a pattern. Call, and we plan from there. See rat control for the usual sequence.

Snap a photo of the droppings or chew marks and mention it on the phone, which saves time.

Removal without poison.

We set traps or stations along the runway, somewhere pets and kids cannot get to, and we revisit them on a timetable. We prefer that to scattering bait, which can reach pets and wildlife and leave dead animals in walls. See trapping and removal.

Once catches slow, we close the entries and talk about habits. A yard visit is described on our backyard page.

A simple evening routine.

  • Pick up fruit and sweep spilled seed.
  • Bring pet bowls inside and close the food tub.
  • Check the compost lid.
  • Turn off hoses and fix any drip.
  • Look at the roof edge for new gaps or limbs.

It takes five minutes and does more than most traps.

Shared fences and neighbors.

Sometimes the draw is next door. A neighbor's fruit tree, a feeder or an overgrown corner can feed rats that then visit you. You cannot fix another yard, but you can have a friendly word, share this guide and keep your own side tight.

Rats do not stop at property lines, so a joint effort holds better than a solo one. If several yards on a block clean up fruit and lid their seed, the whole block loses its easy meal.

When to call.

Call if you see rat sign, hear steps on the roof or find burrows. Tell us about your trees, feeders and fence, and we start in the yard. A person answers at every hour.

What to do first: CDC-based cleanup steps

Return to this routine each time you find pellets, urine marks or torn nesting inside the house.

  • A shop vac seems thorough yet may push tiny particles back out into the room.
  • In any shut-up space, fresh air comes ahead of every other step, and half an hour is the common guideline.
  • Gloves guard you while you work, and soap guards you once you stop.
  • Disinfect first, collect afterward, once the soaked waste has stood for about five minutes.
  • Close the bag, and keep skin well clear of the animal.

Follow-up questions.

Must my fruit tree come down?

No. Gather windfall fruit often, pick ripe fruit promptly and trim any limbs resting on the roof.

Should I stop feeding birds?

You do not have to. Use a tray, store seed in a lidded bin and sweep up spills each evening.

How do I know it is a rat, not a squirrel?

Squirrels are active in daylight. Rats move at dusk and night, and leave larger droppings and gnawed fruit.

Can I compost safely?

A closed bin helps. Avoid meat, oil and dairy, and turn the pile.

Does a pet bowl outside cause trouble?

It can. Pick it up at night and store food in a lidded container.
