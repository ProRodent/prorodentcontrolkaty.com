After the Storm: Rodents and Heavy Rain in Katy

Gulf Coast storms bring sudden, heavy rain and plenty of debris. Both can shift where rodents shelter, and a walk around the house afterward can catch trouble early.

Yes, heavy rain and storm cleanup can send rodents toward your house. Water floods burrows, debris piles up as cover and storm damage opens gaps. The fix starts with a quick walk around the outside and a short list of things to check.

How rain changes what animals do.

Rats and mice that nest in the ground lose their burrows when water rises. They look for higher, drier cover, and a garage, shed or attic can fit. Roof rats already live above the ground, but a soaked yard may send them looking for food along fences and wires.

None of this is certain for any one house. It is a pattern worth knowing, because it explains why noises sometimes start a few days after a big rain and not during it.

The walk-around after a storm.

Pick a dry hour and take a flashlight. Walk the outside of the house slowly, and note anything that looks different from last week.

Debris is cover.

Branches, leaves and soaked boards stacked against a wall give rodents a hiding place right where they want it. Clear debris off the foundation and away from the garage door as soon as it is safe.

Before reaching into a pile, wear gloves and look. If you see droppings or nest material, stop and read the safety steps at the bottom of this page. Droppings should be wetted, not swept.

Check the places storms do not reach.

Water can also find its way into the attic through damaged roofing, and wet insulation attracts nesting animals. If you see stains on a ceiling or smell mustiness, call a roofer for the leak and an inspection for the animals. Both problems can be present at once.

  • Look at the ceiling for new stains or soft spots.
  • Check the attic hatch for droppings or pulled insulation.
  • Test the garage door seal after the water recedes.
  • Check vehicles that sat through the storm for nests.
  • Smell the closets near the air handler for mustiness or a sharp odor.

Rodents settle in fast, and an early call usually costs less than fixing damage later.

Timing: when signs appear.

Rodents do not announce themselves during a storm. Noises tend to begin a few days later, often at night, after the ground starts to dry. Droppings show up in the garage or the pantry. If you hear steps overhead, note the hour and the room.

Early action is cheaper. A gap closed this week costs less than the repair of a chewed duct next month.

Closing what the storm opened.

Temporary patches help for a day or two. Lasting repairs use metal, mesh and sealant. Exclusion work covers the roof edge, vents and garage, and a yard visit handles burrows and cover. If animals are already inside, trapping comes first.

If a storm took out power for a while, check the freezer and refrigerator for spoiled food, since smells and scraps attract animals. Seal waste in a lidded bin until pickup.

When to phone.

Call if you find droppings, gnawing or burrows, hear steps overhead or smell something unpleasant. A person takes the call at every hour, and we may be able to come the same day.

Safety guidance built on CDC advice

Everything here repeats guidance from public health bodies, so any household can use it whether or not it ever books a service.

  • Keep compressed air, brooms and dry cloths out of any job involving droppings or soiled nesting.
  • A garage, shed or crawl space that has sat closed should get roughly half an hour of through-draft before you enter.
  • Start with gloves on and end with a hand wash.
  • Drench each pellet and stain in disinfectant, and give it roughly five minutes before touching any of it.
  • One securely closed bag is enough to carry droppings, towels and a carcass outside.
  • When fever or shortness of breath appears, phone your own doctor first and describe the exposure.

More to clear up.

Do storms push rodents into houses?

They can. Flooded burrows and wet ground make dry, sheltered spaces look better, and storm damage can open new gaps.

What should I check right after a storm?

Look at the foundation, vent covers, the garage door seal and the roof edge for new gaps, torn screens and piled debris.

Is it safe to clean up storm debris myself?

Wear gloves and avoid reaching into piles blindly. If you see droppings or nests, use the wet method on the CDC page linked below.

How long after a storm might I notice animals?

Days to weeks. Signs often show up as noises at night or droppings in the garage after the water recedes.

Should I call if I find nothing?

Not necessarily. A short note in your phone and another look in a week is reasonable. Call if anything changes.
