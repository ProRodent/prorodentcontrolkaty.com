How we read a Katy property.

Katy is a home-rule city at the point where Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties come together, built on coastal prairie once farmed for rice. County lines matter for mail and permits. For a rodent problem, the structure and the soil around it count for much more.

The upper Texas coast has long, steamy summers and sudden downpours, and rodents respond to both. Heat pushes them into cool, shaded voids like garage corners and soffits, while a hard rain can drive burrowers off low ground to drier cover. A ten-minute walk outside with a torch shows which factors apply to you.

Mouse, rat or something else?

A house mouse fits through a hole only slightly wider than a ballpoint pen, whereas a rat wants roughly half an inch. Roof rats climb, using fences, cables and limbs to get to the eaves, and Norway rats prefer to dig alongside slabs and sheds.

Species determines which trap, which sealing material and which cleanup method fit the job, so we identify the animal before drawing up a plan. Your phone description gives us a head start, and the walk-through supplies what is missing.

Heat, rain and the calendar.

Most rodent travel happens in darkness, which is why signs surface at night. A string of scorching afternoons or a stormy week can shift where animals shelter. Name the room where you heard it and about when the sound started.

  • Inspect vent covers, pipe collars and the lower corners of the garage door each spring and again ahead of fall storms.
  • Store pet kibble, birdseed and grass seed in tubs that close tightly.
  • Rack firewood and lumber clear of the walls.
  • Cut back any branch that touches the roof or rests on the siding.

What to do until the technician gets there.

Four small habits keep the household safe and the evidence intact. Leave droppings unswept, keep children and pets out of that room, and let any traps you already placed stay exactly where they are.

  • Photograph droppings, chew marks and damage, placing a coin beside each to indicate scale.
  • Write down the room and the hour of every noise.
  • Close the door to the worst-affected room.
  • Skip loose bait and glue boards, which bring their own hazards.

What a visit looks like.

One mouse in a pantry or a nest in a truck engine, the visit follows the same four steps.

  1. 1

    Look.

    The technician begins in the attic, garage, crawl space or yard and works along the roofline, searching for fresh evidence and for the openings animals use.

  2. 2

    Remove.

    Traps follow the runways the evidence reveals, the style matched to the species, and each device is placed out of range of kids and animals at home.

  3. 3

    Seal.

    Gaps, vents and chewed holes get shut with flashing, mesh and a sealant rodents cannot chew.

  4. 4

    Verify.

    A return trip examines the traps, tests the seals and handles whatever cleanup or repair remains.

Clues that rodents have moved in.

When two or more of these appear in the same corner of the house, animals are probably active now and did not simply pass through long ago.

  • Pellets.

    Dark, rice-sized droppings turning up beside a baseboard, within a drawer, atop a garage shelf or over attic insulation.

  • Fresh gnawing.

    New tooth marks appear light-colored and ragged, whether the target was timber, PVC, cable sheathing or a cereal box.

  • Sounds at night.

    Scratching, scurrying or a dull thump behind a wall or over the ceiling after the house settles.

  • Nest material.

    Shredded paper, tugged insulation, bits of cloth or dry grass stuffed into a protected corner.

  • Greasy runs.

    Oily dark smears along walls, pipes and joists show a path used night after night.

  • A smell.

    A sharp urine odor in a shut room, or a deeper rotten stench where an animal has died.

Answers for Katy households.

Mouse or rat: how do I know which?

Start with the droppings. Tiny ones in kitchen drawers suggest mice, while chunkier ones along a roofline or over attic insulation suggest rats. A hole dug next to a slab or shed also points to rats.

How do rodents enter a house?

A mouse can squeeze past a slit as wide as a pencil, and a rat wants about twice that. Common entries include openings where pipes and cables cross walls, vent covers, garage door corners, foundation vents and the roof edge.

Can trapping solve it alone?

Traps clear out animals already indoors, while sealing stops new ones. Most jobs call for both, in that order, plus a return trip to verify the result.

Is the work safe for kids and pets at home?

Tell us who shares the home before work begins. Traps and stations are placed where nobody can reach them, and afterward we report what was placed and where.

Which county is Katy in?

Parts of three: Harris, Fort Bend and Waller. Whether we cover a Katy address comes down to its ZIP and the street you state on the call, so a county line never holds up a booking.

Is Cinco Ranch inside Katy?

No. Cinco Ranch is a census-designated place southeast of the city of Katy, zoned to Katy ISD. It has its own page here, and we take calls from it by street address.
