Start with the question you actually have.

Most people who call for an inspection do not know what they have. They know the evidence: a smell near the garage, a sound above the ceiling, droppings in a drawer. Our job is to turn that evidence into a name, a route and a plan.

That order matters. Trapping the wrong animal wastes money, and sealing before you know where the entry is can lock a rodent inside the wall. A short, honest look first costs less than a wrong guess.

The route through the property.

We start outside, because most rodents arrive from the ground, a fence or a tree. The technician checks the foundation, vents, roof edge and any limb that touches the house, then moves inside to the garage, the attic and any under-floor space.

  • Exterior: foundation, vents, soffits, roof edge, fences, trees and burrows.
  • Garage: door seals, wall base, shelving and stored boxes.
  • Attic: insulation tunnels, droppings, rub marks and the area around the hatch.
  • Interior: cabinets, appliance gaps and any wall where sound is reported.

Where something is hard to reach, we photograph it and show you the picture. You see the same evidence we do.

Not every sound is a rodent. Ducts expand in heat, loose flashing taps in wind and squirrels run on the roof in daylight. We rule those out so you do not pay to chase the wrong problem.

Reference points we ask about.

When we book, we ask for the nearest cross street and any landmark you use, such as Katy Heritage Park, VFW Park or the MKT Railroad Museum. We know those places by name only, and we use them to picture the area, not to claim anything about them.

What helps more is the building: slab or raised floor, one story or two, fence type, and whether a drainage ditch or open field runs behind the lot.

Give us your street and a few details, and we will look for the earliest opening.

What you get at the end.

You get the animal's name, the entries we found, plus a plan with a price attached to each stage. If the answer is that you have a small problem, we say so. If the answer is that several steps are needed, we put them in the order that makes sense and mark which can wait.

Some customers stop after the inspection and handle the sealing themselves. That is a reasonable choice, and the written notes help you do it.

Handy to mention.

  • What you noticed and the day it started.
  • Where the attic hatch or crawl door is.
  • Whether pets will be in the house during the visit.
  • Any earlier trapping, bait or sealing you tried.

Do these ahead of time.

  • If a pickup has nesting stuff under the hood, leave it parked until inspected.
  • Move lawn seed, kibble and bird food inside for the time being.
  • Save any gnawed item you find in a bag for the technician.
  • Find the main electrical panel in case a cable has been chewed.

What to do during the wait, and what to leave alone

Read through every line before handling anything, because a short pause spares your lungs.

  • Keep compressed air, brooms and dry cloths out of any job involving droppings or soiled nesting.
  • Unseen hazards can linger in stale air, so open the space, step away and give it half an hour.
  • Since germs cannot be seen, begin with gloves and end with soap and water.
  • Drench each pellet and stain in disinfectant, and give it roughly five minutes before touching any of it.
  • Close the bag, and keep skin well clear of the animal.
  • A physician alone can weigh how serious an exposure was, so reach out to one should fever or labored breathing set in.

Inspection questions, answered.

How much time does a walk-through need?

An average home usually takes under an hour, while a big property or an awkward attic can run longer. We tell you the expected length when we book.

Will I get a price before any work starts?

Yes. The visit ends with a written plan and a price for each stage, and nothing begins until you agree.

What if you find no rodents?

Then we say so, and explain what else might be making the noise, such as ducts expanding in heat or a loose piece of flashing.

Do I need to be home?

An adult needs to be on hand to open the attic, garage and yard. Findings can be discussed right there.

Can you inspect a vehicle?

Yes. Tell us where it is parked, and we inspect the engine bay, the air filter box and the cabin ventilation.
