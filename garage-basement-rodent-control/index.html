Why the garage draws animals.

A garage offers shelter, quiet and soft material for nests. In a hot, humid place it also offers shade, and a closed door keeps out the worst of the sun. Add pet food in a bin with a loose lid or a bag of birdseed, and the room becomes a meal.

Because we rarely spend time in garages, a problem can run for weeks. Many owners notice only when a car will not start, a tote has a hole or the smell reaches the kitchen.

We also test the garage door itself. A door that does not close fully because of a bent track or a worn spring leaves a gap along the whole width.

Clutter is the hidden helper.

Stacked cardboard, old rags and unused cushions are nesting material. Heavy shelving against a wall hides the base of the wall, which is where animals enter. You do not have to clear everything, only reduce what touches the floor and the wall.

  • Move food items into sealed plastic or metal tubs.
  • Lift stored boxes onto shelves, at least a few inches off the floor.
  • Pull large items a hand's width from walls so the wall base can be seen.
  • Replace cardboard with plastic tubs for long-term storage.

Vehicles and equipment.

A truck or car parked in the garage can pick up a nest in the engine bay. If you find chewed wires, shredded insulation under the hood or droppings on the air filter housing, read the vehicle removal page before you start the engine.

Lawn equipment, bicycles and a spare refrigerator are all worth a look. A freezer motor can hold nesting material and a damaged cord is a fire risk.

Snap a photo of the droppings or chew marks and mention it on the phone, which saves time.

From removal to sealing.

We start with trapping along the wall base and near stored items. Once catches slow, exclusion closes the door, wall and pipe gaps. If the garage connects to an attic, we check that space for droppings and tunnels as well.

Basements are less common in this part of Texas, but if you have one, we check window wells, stair openings and the rim joist in the same way.

Information to have nearby.

  • How the garage door meets the slab.
  • What leans against the walls or sits on the floor.
  • Whether anything has been stored untouched for months.
  • A basement or lower storage room, if the home has one.

What to do in the interim.

  • Open a path toward the garage wall, the sink base cabinet and the attic access.
  • Do not shake out gnawed cardboard boxes, as that puts dust in the air.
  • If snap traps go out, set them far from toddlers and large dogs.
  • Think about which room smells strongest and how you would describe the odor.

Safety guidance built on CDC advice

Return to this routine each time you find pellets, urine marks or torn nesting inside the house.

  • A shop vac seems thorough yet may push tiny particles back out into the room.
  • Leave a closed-up area with its doors wide for half an hour prior to starting.
  • Since germs cannot be seen, begin with gloves and end with soap and water.
  • Sequence counts: wet the waste, let it stand about five minutes, and only then wipe, which keeps dust from rising.
  • Tie the bag shut and put it with ordinary household trash. Lifting a dead rodent with bare fingers is never acceptable.
  • We offer no medical claims. If fever, muscle aches or labored breathing follow contact with waste, consult a physician.

Garage questions from Katy callers.

Why do rodents like garages?

A garage is usually shaded, rarely disturbed and full of cardboard, fabric and paper. If the door seal is worn, the space is easy to enter and hard to notice.

Does a cooled garage change anything?

It can make the space more attractive in summer. An uncooled one can be very hot, and animals may prefer shaded spots under shelves and behind boxes.

Can a garage problem reach the house?

Yes. Wall voids, ducts and shared attics can connect the garage to the living space, so we check both.

What should I move before you arrive?

Pull boxes a short way from the walls, if it is easy, so we can see the wall base. Do not sweep droppings.

Do you work in basements?

Yes, where a home has one. Tell us about stairs, window wells and any damp spots, since those affect entries.

Garage visits around Katy.

Tell us how the garage door meets the slab, what leans on the walls and whether anything stays stored for months. If the home also has a basement or a lower storage room, say so.
