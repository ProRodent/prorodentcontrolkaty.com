How rats find a Katy house.

Roof rats and Norway rats behave differently, and either may turn up on this part of the Gulf Coast. Roof rats climb, so they favor fences, cables, vines and limbs that lead to the eaves. Norway rats dig, so they favor soft ground beside a slab, shed or retaining edge.

Katy sits on coastal prairie, and a lot can back onto a fence, a drainage ditch or an open strip of grass. Long, quiet corridors like those can act as travel lanes. The MKT railroad gave Katy its name, and any rail corridor or easement running behind a lot is worth mentioning on the call.

Signs that say rat and not mouse.

  • Droppings larger than a grain of rice, often in groups along rafters or in a corner of the attic.
  • Gnawed fruit, nuts or pet food bags with wide, deep tooth marks.
  • Heavy scampering or thumping overhead just after dusk.
  • Burrow holes about two inches across beside a slab, deck or shed.
  • Dark oily smears on a fence rail, a pipe or a beam.

One sign proves little. Two or three together make a pattern, and a pattern tells us where to start.

Yard first, then roof, then attic.

A rat inspection begins outside because that is where the route lives. The technician follows the fence, notes where branches touch the roof, searches for burrows next to the slab and finishes with the soffits, vents and roof edge.

Remove the animals, then take away the invitation.

Rats are wary, so trap placement matters. We set larger snap traps or enclosed stations along the runway and check them on a schedule you are told about. Once catches slow, exclusion work closes the entry with metal and mesh, because rats chew through foam and wood.

Yard changes help too. Picking up fallen fruit, lidding the compost, raising pet food at night and trimming limbs back from the roof all reduce traffic. The backyard and outdoor page goes through those steps in order.

What to do before we arrive.

Keep kids and pets clear of the attic hatch and the shed, and do not disturb droppings. Note when you hear the noise, since the hour hints at whether the animal enters from the roof or from the ground.

Planning to set your own traps? Please hold off until we talk. A trap placed in the wrong spot can educate a rat and make the real job harder.

When the job is finished.

Quiet nights and a clean trap line are the first sign. Soiled insulation calls for attic cleanup, and chewed ducts or wiring go to damage repair. We note each repair separately so the cost is easy to read.

Describe to the dispatcher.

  • The time and place the rat showed itself or was heard.
  • Tall trees, fences, cables or a shed close to the roofline.
  • The droppings you found and roughly how big.
  • Fruit trees, a compost bin or a feeder in the yard.

Until we arrive.

  • If a pickup has nesting stuff under the hood, leave it parked until inspected.
  • Move lawn seed, kibble and bird food inside for the time being.
  • Save any gnawed item you find in a bag for the technician.
  • Find the main electrical panel in case a cable has been chewed.

Safety steps for dealing with waste and nests

General information drawn from federal health pages; your own physician settles any medical question.

  • Keep compressed air, brooms and dry cloths out of any job involving droppings or soiled nesting.
  • Shut-in rooms hold stale air, so throw open doors and windows and wait outside about thirty minutes.
  • Hands stay covered throughout, then get a thorough wash.
  • Drench each pellet and stain in disinfectant, and give it roughly five minutes before touching any of it.
  • Droppings and wipes share one tight bag, and your skin never meets a dead animal.
  • We offer no medical claims. If fever, muscle aches or labored breathing follow contact with waste, consult a physician.

Rat questions from Katy callers.

How do I tell a rat from a mouse?

Check the scale first. Rat pellets match a hefty rice kernel or exceed it, while mouse pellets are small and tapered. Digging at a slab edge and fruit scarred by deep bites also hint at rats.

Can rats settle in an attic here?

Yes. Roof rats are adept climbers that can follow branches, fences or cables up to the roofline, and heavy steps overhead around dusk are a frequent complaint.

Is it wise to block a hole myself?

Wait for the technician. A hole closed around a live animal may drive it farther inside, and a rushed plug often gets chewed out again.

Will cutting back a tree help?

Often, yes. A limb that touches the roof is a bridge, and trimming it removes one route. For large trees, hire a tree service, and we will tell you which limbs matter.

Do you handle rats at night?

Yes. A person takes the call at all hours, and we can often send someone out the same day if the calendar allows.
