Heat, rain and your walls.

Spring is nesting time. Rodents that spent the cold months inside produce litters within insulation and wall voids, and a house that sounded calm in March can be busy by May, so an early call keeps the cleanup smaller.

Questions for Fulshear callers.

Our records lack a street-level portrait of Fulshear, so we offer no description of its homes and ask questions instead. Is the lot fenced, do trees reach the roof, is there a shed or a detached garage, and does a vehicle sit unused for long stretches?

Warm, wet weather sends animals hunting for a dry, shady nook. Garages, attics and crawl spaces all qualify, and a gap left alone in spring turns into a doorway by the time heat or rain arrives.

Sheds, workshops and detached garages.

An outbuilding often becomes the first stop for an animal and the base from which it works toward the house. If you have a shed or workshop, say if its floor is slab or dirt, and whether seed, feed, firewood or paint cans are kept inside.

Detached garages are worth the same attention. A door that does not meet the slab, a loose side door and shelving pushed against the wall all help animals settle in, and each is quick to check during the first walk-through.

Noises, smells and mess.

Any one item below justifies a call, and two at once suggest current activity. Write down when each clue first turned up, then dial.

  • Attic noise following a stretch of heavy rain.
  • A rotting smell with nothing in sight to explain it.
  • Torn insulation or crushed flexible duct.
  • Droppings next to the air handler or water heater.
  • Worn tracks along a fence line or foundation.

Safety checklist for rodent droppings

No call is needed to follow the list below, since it mirrors what health agencies tell every homeowner.

  • Get dry waste damp before anything contacts it, and put the broom down.
  • Prop windows and doors open, stay outdoors for about thirty minutes, and let the air turn over.
  • Single-use or reusable gloves both do the job, so long as the gloves and your hands are washed after.
  • Cover the droppings plus the patch under them, wait about five minutes, and blot with throwaway towels.
  • Put used towels and any dead rodent into a plastic bag, and keep bare skin off the body.
  • We offer no medical claims. If fever, muscle aches or labored breathing follow contact with waste, consult a physician.
