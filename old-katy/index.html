What we can say about Old Katy, and what we cannot.

Public sources describe "Old Towne Katy" as the portion of the city that was incorporated in 1945, and they mention a historic town square on the former MKT railroad right-of-way. That is the extent of what we could verify. We found no surveyed boundary, street list or building dates that we could stand behind, so this page follows the commonly used name and adds nothing invented. Whatever the history, the inspection starts at the roofline and the foundation, and your own building is what we read.

In practical terms, tell us your street or the nearest cross street when you call. We use that to plan the first visit and to decide how much of the lot to check.

Rail corridors, fences and open strips.

Katy took its name from the "K-T" initials of the MKT railroad, which is why a rail line is part of its story. Rodents do not care about history, but they do like long, quiet edges where they can travel without crossing open ground. A rail corridor, a drainage easement or a fence line with tall grass can all serve.

Does your lot back onto an edge like that? Tell us which side of it faces that edge. We cannot change what lies beyond the fence, but we can seal your side and reduce what draws animals to it.

Raised floors, roofs and older materials.

We cannot tell you which houses in Old Katy have raised floors or how old any roof is. If yours does, the space beneath is worth a look. Vents and screens there can tear in wind or rot in damp weather, and a torn one leaves an open door.

Older materials are not a problem in themselves. What matters is whether they have gaps. Wood trim that has pulled away from siding, a vent cover that has cracked or a pipe hole cut too large all deserve a patch. See entry point sealing for how we close them.

Shops, homes and shared walls.

If you run a business or rent a unit near the center of town, the questions change a little. Who handles the dumpster? Do deliveries come to a shared back door? Is there a wall that adjoins another tenant? Rodents travel through voids, so a fix works best when neighbors take part.

  • Keep back doors shut except during deliveries.
  • Seal gaps where pipes and cables cross a shared wall.
  • Use bins with lids that fit, and rinse them now and then.
  • Settle on a collection schedule so trash bags do not sit out overnight.

The commercial page describes recurring checks and the written record we leave after every stop.

Heat, storms and the calendar.

Summers here are hot and humid, and heavy rain can arrive quickly. Rodents shift where they shelter in response. A cooled building can attract animals in July, and a soaked yard can push burrowers toward dry cover. After a storm, take a short walk around the foundation and look for new holes, loose vents or debris against the wall.

If you hear noise in the ceiling or smell something unpleasant, call at any hour. Someone answers, and we can often come the same day if there is space on the calendar.

Before you call: handling droppings safely

Aimed at residents, this list holds dust down while you wait for a technician.

  • Aged pellets turn to powder that people breathe in, so leave them unswept.
  • Unseen hazards can linger in stale air, so open the space, step away and give it half an hour.
  • At minimum wear gloves while working, and wash when the job is finished.
  • Sequence counts: wet the waste, let it stand about five minutes, and only then wipe, which keeps dust from rising.
  • Tie the bag shut and put it with ordinary household trash. Lifting a dead rodent with bare fingers is never acceptable.
  • Flu-type symptoms after a cleanup should be mentioned to a physician, since diagnosing them is not our work.
