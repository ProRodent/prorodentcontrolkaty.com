What Cinco Ranch is, and what it is not.

Public sources describe Cinco Ranch as a census-designated place in Fort Bend and Harris counties. It is about ten miles southeast of Katy, it is not part of the city of Katy or its extraterritorial jurisdiction, and it is zoned to Katy ISD. People often group it with Greater Katy. We could not confirm a ZIP code, a street list or any landmark inside it, so we state none. The page follows the common name, not a surveyed boundary.

The practical meaning is simple. Call with your street address, and we confirm coverage on the call. We do not guess from the city name on your mail.

Reading a property without a neighborhood map.

Because we are not describing streets or housing, we ask questions instead. Is the lot fenced? Do trees touch the roof? Is there a pond, a drainage channel or a greenbelt behind the fence? Does the garage door sit tight to the slab? The answers shape the first visit more than any area name could.

Edges, water and fences.

Rodents travel along edges because open ground feels exposed. A fence line, the border of a pond or a strip of tall grass gives them a path. If your lot touches one, we pay extra attention to the fence base, the foundation line and any shed.

We do not know what any particular edge in Cinco Ranch looks like, and we will not guess. If you describe it on the call, we plan around what you say.

Garages, attics and the cooling system.

In a hot climate, a garage that stays shaded and a cooled attic both draw animals. Check the garage door seal, the vents on the roof and the screens on the soffit. Noises that peak after sunset often come from the attic or a wall void near the garage.

  • Look at the bottom corners of the garage door for daylight.
  • Check that roof and soffit vents have intact screens.
  • Keep pet food in lidded tubs and bring bowls in at night.
  • Gather dropped fruit and sweep up seed spilled under feeders.

See the garage page and the exclusion page for the work in detail.

Storms, heat and timing.

Heavy rain can push animals off low ground and toward higher, drier cover such as a roofline or a garage. Hot spells make shade and cool air valuable. After storms, look for new burrows, debris against the foundation and torn vent covers.

The line is answered day and night. For addresses at the far edge of our range, we give an honest arrival window, and we try to combine tasks so one trip does as much as it can.

What to tell us.

Give the street address, say whether the lot touches water, a greenbelt or an open field, and describe the fence and any shed. Mention pets and children. If you have noticed noise or a smell, tell us when it began.

Facts that save time.

  • Your full street address.
  • Whether the lot borders water, a greenbelt or open land.
  • Fence type and any shed or play structure.
  • Garage door condition and attic noise.

What to do in the interim.

  • Close the door on the worst-affected room and leave it closed.
  • Dry pellets stay put; sweeping them lifts dust you do not want in your lungs.
  • Hardware-store bait can send a poisoned animal off to die in a duct or wall cavity.
  • Note the clock time when scratching starts, because timing says a lot about the species.

Before you sweep, spray or bag anything

Aimed at residents, this list holds dust down while you wait for a technician.

  • Never dry-sweep, because a broom flings fine particles up to face height.
  • Air the space out first: open every door and window that opens, then give it thirty minutes.
  • Hands stay covered throughout, then get a thorough wash.
  • Leave droppings alone until they are drenched and have sat for around five minutes.
  • Droppings and wipes share one tight bag, and your skin never meets a dead animal.
  • Fever, aches or breathlessness following contact with waste belong with a clinic rather than an exterminator.
