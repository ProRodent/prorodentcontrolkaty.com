Step by step.

Heavy droppings and nests call for cleanup, though only after removal ends. When trapping wraps up, attic and crawl space decontamination restores the space, and it never comes first. When the walk-through reveals a smaller problem than you dreaded, you hear that as well.

Early signs in plain words.

Roughly in order from quiet clues to loud ones, the list is easy to scan. Pick the matches and bring them up on the call.

  • Tapping or chewing in a soffit after dark.
  • Insulation pulled loose around the edge of a hatch.
  • Droppings across a garage shelf of totes.
  • A burnt smell as you start a parked vehicle.
  • Rub marks on a fence rail or pipe.

We start with three details: which room, what time the sound occurs and what it sounds like.

Details that shape the plan.

With a city this large, we cannot say anything useful about a typical Houston property. The questions are the same ones we ask everywhere. House, townhouse or apartment? Shared walls? A garage? A yard with trees or a fence that touches the roof?

A garage behaves like a side door. Door seals flatten, screens rip and totes supply nesting fiber, yet hardly anyone inspects those rooms, so trouble can build quietly for weeks.

Townhouses, apartments and shared walls.

In multi-unit buildings, the plan has to account for neighbors. Rodents move through wall voids, pipe chases and shared attics, so a single treated unit may not hold. If you rent, give the manager the details of what you found, because sealing works best when it lines up across units.

Detached homes bring other questions: fences, fruit trees, a shed and a driveway that holds a parked vehicle. The yard and the vehicle pages go through those, and the first call helps us decide which applies.

Looking ahead to storm season.

Dry spells are ideal for sealing the outside of a building. Dropped fruit, a pet dish left on the patio and a boat or trailer in storage can lure animals too, so tidy the grounds while conditions allow.

How soon we can arrive.

Hearing a scratch overhead at midnight is a fine reason to dial at midnight. A person stays on the line all night, and same-day trips to Houston are booked whenever the schedule permits.

Small jobs for the wait.

  • Skip the attic and the under-floor space, because shifting insulation sends grit airborne.
  • A nest is best left alone, because a frightened animal may burrow farther in.
  • Locate the attic hatch and crawl-space door in advance, so the visit starts quickly.
  • A truck showing nest debris is better rolled out of the garage if that is simple.

Safety steps for dealing with waste and nests

Going slowly at the start keeps fine dust out of your airways.

  • The first rule is simple: no brushing or vacuuming of anything dry.
  • Unseen hazards can linger in stale air, so open the space, step away and give it half an hour.
  • Hands stay covered throughout, then get a thorough wash.
  • Cover the droppings plus the patch under them, wait about five minutes, and blot with throwaway towels.
  • Tie the bag shut and put it with ordinary household trash. Lifting a dead rodent with bare fingers is never acceptable.
  • Keep an eye on your health during the following weeks. If a fever or shortness of breath appears, give your doctor the exact details of what you touched.
